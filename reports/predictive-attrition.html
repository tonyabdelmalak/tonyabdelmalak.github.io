Predicting Turnover: AI-Driven Attrition Risk

High turnover was draining institutional knowledge and driving up costs. This model flags employees at risk of voluntary departure so HR teams can step in early, with the model supporting human judgment, not replacing it.

Data and method

  • Data: anonymized HRIS fields including tenure, performance ratings, compensation bands, engagement survey scores, promotion history and absence patterns. Personal identifiers removed.
  • Features: cleaned missing values, normalized numeric fields, encoded categories and engineered signals such as time since last promotion.
  • Model: compared logistic regression, random forests and gradient boosting; chose a random forest for its balance of accuracy and interpretability. Final F1 0.72, AUC 0.81 on a held-out test set.

Ethics

Sensitive attributes (age, gender, ethnicity) were excluded from the production model and it was audited for disparate impact. Risk scores fed a broader retention conversation, never an automatic decision, and the work was reviewed with legal and privacy partners.

Results and lessons

HR teams aimed stay interviews and development opportunities at people flagged as high risk. Within six months, voluntary attrition was down 28% against the prior year, and the model surfaced systemic issues, such as a lack of mentoring, that shaped broader programs.

  • Data quality matters. A reliable pipeline mattered as much as the model.
  • Collaboration is the job. HR partners, data engineers and leaders had to trust the output.
  • Models drift. Retraining and monitoring were scheduled from day one.
