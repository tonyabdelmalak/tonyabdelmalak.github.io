Measuring Engagement Impact

Engagement surveys are everywhere. The question is whether the score actually moves productivity or turnover. This page lets the data answer, department by department.

Engagement and productivity

Productivity by engagement score

Correlation by department

In this sample, engagement barely tracks productivity anywhere: every department is between −0.19 and +0.07. The original page called Marketing’s 0.07 a sign of “noticeable productivity gains”; the data doesn’t support that, so the claim is gone. Engineering’s slight negative relationship is the one worth a closer look.

Monthly turnover

Turnover rate by month

Turnover in this synthetic sample is unrealistically high (around 50% a month), so read it for shape, not level.

Takeaway

A survey score on its own is a weak lever. Pair engagement with the things it should change (manager quality, workload, growth) and test interventions, rather than assuming a higher score means higher output.

About the data. Every point is from the synthetic sample embedded in the original interactive page. Correlations are recomputed from those points; the original page only quoted Marketing’s figure (0.07), which matches.
