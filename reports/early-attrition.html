Case study · Predictive and prescriptive analytics

Reducing 90-Day Attrition

After a stretch of fast hiring, early attrition spiked. Leadership had a limited budget and a real question: redesign onboarding, train managers, offer incentives, or some mix?

What changed

Before and after the interventions

Early attrition is shown in percent; reporting effort and ramp-up are indexed so that before = 100.

90-day attrition after rollout

Attrition fell from 35% to 25% within six months of the onboarding redesign and manager enablement.

Which levers worked

Estimated uplift by intervention

Mentor assignment and a structured first day did the most for early-career cohorts; manager training mattered most where specific managers were the issue.

Data and method

  • Data foundation: HRIS, ATS, LMS, survey and scheduling data joined into one governed dataset with PII masking, role-based access and small-cell suppression.
  • Predictive: survival analysis (Cox proportional hazards) to see when risk peaks, and gradient boosting with SHAP values to surface drivers such as first-week schedule adherence, mentor assignment and pay-band misalignment.
  • Uplift: a T-learner estimated the causal effect of each intervention by segment.
  • Prescriptive: a linear program allocated budget across sites and cohorts to maximize retained days within cost and fairness limits. It recommended about 60% to onboarding redesign for the highest-risk cohorts and 40% to manager training.

Model outputs

The original analysis outputs, shown as published (synthetic data).

Results

Within one quarter of launch, early attrition fell 28% across the pilot groups, manual reporting time dropped about 40% thanks to automated pipelines and scorecards, and new-hire ramp-up improved 17% on productivity KPIs. Leaders got a clear answer on where to invest.
